Overview

The lecture covers the legal and ethical aspects of confidentiality and documentation in social work. Key legislation such as the Public Access to Information and Secrecy Act, the Social Services Act and the LSS is reviewed, together with fundamental principles for handling sensitive personal data in a legally secure way.

Key points
Confidentiality entails a duty to protect sensitive information about individuals, both spoken and written.
Confidentiality may only be broken where the law requires it, for example when a child is suspected of being at risk.
Documentation is essential for ensuring quality, legal certainty and continuity in social work.
Ethical principles such as respect for individual self-determination are balanced against society's need for transparency.
Key terms
Public Access to Information and Secrecy Act
Governs when information may be disclosed or must be kept confidential within public-sector bodies.
Social Services Act (SoL)
Governs the work of the social services, including confidentiality and documentation.
GDPR
The EU's data protection regulation governing the processing of personal data.
Principle of public access
The public's right to access official documents held by public authorities, with exceptions for classified information.
Security and data protection
Data within the EU

AI models in Europe
Transcription runs in Finland, summaries and search in France.
As little data as possible
